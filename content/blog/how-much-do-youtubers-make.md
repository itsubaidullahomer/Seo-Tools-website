There is no single figure, because a YouTuber's ad income is views divided by 1,000, multiplied by the channel's RPM, and RPM is commonly reported anywhere from under $1 to well above $10 depending on niche, audience country and video format. Public roundups commonly quote long-form RPM at roughly $2 to $10, which would make 100,000 views worth about $200 to $1,000 before any sponsorships. Channels earn nothing from ads until they are accepted into the YouTube Partner Program.

*Last reviewed September 30, 2026.*

## Quick answer

- **Formula:** monthly ad income ≈ monthly views ÷ 1,000 × RPM.
- **Commonly quoted RPM:** roughly $2 to $10 for long-form video in public roundups. These are not YouTube statistics.
- **Creator share:** YouTube's published terms have generally described creators keeping 55% of net long-form ad revenue. Confirm the current wording in YouTube Help.
- **Eligibility:** ad revenue starts only after joining the Partner Program.
- **Payment:** money is paid once your balance passes a threshold, usually $100.

## CPM, RPM and CPC: three numbers that get mixed up

Most wrong earnings estimates use the wrong one of these.

| Term | Stands for | Counted per | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CPM | Cost per mille | 1,000 ads shown | What advertisers pay, before YouTube's share |
| RPM | Revenue per mille | 1,000 views of any kind | What the channel earned, after the share |
| CPC | Cost per click | One click on an ad | What an advertiser pays per click |

CPM is the advertiser's side. In YouTube Analytics the related figure is the playback-based CPM, which counts only playbacks that showed at least one ad. RPM is the creator's side. It is spread across all views, including those with no ad, so it runs lower than CPM. Depending on the report, Studio may also fold Premium, memberships and Super Chat into RPM.

CPC is not how Partner Program creators are paid: they receive a share of net ad revenue, not a fee per click. Google's advertising policies treat clicking your own ads, or asking viewers to, as invalid activity, and it can cost a channel its revenue or account.

## From views to dollars: a worked example

These figures are assumptions chosen to make the arithmetic easy to follow, not measurements of any channel. A channel gets 100,000 views in a month. The playback-based CPM is $8, 70% of views include an ad, and the creator share is 55%.

1. Views that showed an ad: 100,000 × 0.70 = 70,000.
2. What advertisers paid: 70,000 ÷ 1,000 × $8 = $560.
3. The creator's share: $560 × 0.55 = $308.
4. RPM: $308 ÷ 100,000 × 1,000 = $3.08.

The shortcut is RPM = CPM × share of views with ads × creator share: 8 × 0.70 × 0.55 = $3.08. Multiplying all 100,000 views by the CPM would give $800, about 2.6 times the real figure.

To find your own RPM, divide revenue by views and multiply by 1,000. If Studio shows $412.50 over 95,000 views, your RPM is $4.34. ## Planning ranges: what a month can look like

This table multiplies monthly views by four RPM levels. It is a set of planning assumptions for judging scale, not YouTube data and not a forecast.

| Monthly views | RPM $1 | RPM $3 | RPM $6 | RPM $12 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | $10 | $30 | $60 | $120 |
| 50,000 | $50 | $150 | $300 | $600 |
| 250,000 | $250 | $750 | $1,500 | $3,000 |
| 1,000,000 | $1,000 | $3,000 | $6,000 | $12,000 |

Each cell is views ÷ 1,000 × RPM. Public roundups tend to place entertainment, vlogs and gaming toward the low end and finance, business and software toward the high end, but they disagree widely and none is official. Replace every preset with your Studio RPM once you have a few months of data.

## Why the same views pay different amounts

Two channels with identical view counts can differ tenfold or more.

- **Niche.** Advertisers pay more to reach people who might buy insurance or software than people watching casual entertainment.
- **Audience country.** If 100,000 views carry a $6 RPM with a mostly high-income audience and $1.50 with a mostly lower-income one, that is $600 versus $150. Studio's geography report shows your split.
- **Video length.** As of this writing, YouTube commonly allows manually placed mid-roll ads only on videos of about eight minutes or longer. At an assumed 150 spoken words per minute that is roughly 1,200 words, and the [words to time converter](/tools/words-to-time) turns any script length into minutes.
- **Season.** Creators commonly report higher RPM late in the year and softer rates early in it. Treat that as a pattern, not a promise.
- **Format.** Shorts are paid through a separate pooled system, and per-view revenue is commonly reported in cents per 1,000 views.

Going from $3.00 to $3.60 is a 20% gain, and the [percentage calculator](/tools/percentage-calculator) compares any two RPMs or months.

## What the Partner Program requires

As of September 30, 2026, the ad-revenue tier is commonly described as 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public watch hours in the past 12 months or 10 million public Shorts views in the past 90 days. A lower tier starts at 500 subscribers with extra conditions and unlocks fan-funding features, not ad revenue. Channels must also follow YouTube's monetization policies and pass a review, and watch time from the Shorts feed is described as not counting toward the 4,000 hours.

YouTube has announced higher thresholds from February 1, 2027, reported as 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views for creators who apply after that date, not for existing partners. That detail rests on press coverage and search summaries of the announcement, so confirm the exact wording and dates before relying on it. Read "YouTube Partner Program overview and eligibility" in YouTube Help for the wording that applies to you.

Watch hours are easier to grasp as views. 4,000 hours is 240,000 minutes, so an average view duration of 4 minutes means 60,000 views. At 2 minutes it is 120,000 views, and at 8 minutes it is 30,000.

## Why most channels earn little, and why averages mislead

This guide found no published YouTube breakdown of channel earnings, so it states no statistic on how many channels earn what. Three points still follow from how the program works: income follows views, every channel below the thresholds earns zero ad revenue by definition, and views on open platforms tend to concentrate in a small share of channels, which the next example imitates.

Take ten hypothetical channels, invented to show the arithmetic, earning $0, $0, $0, $2, $8, $15, $40, $120, $600 and $9,000 a month. The total is $9,785, so the mean is $978.50. The median, the middle of the sorted list, is $11.50. Nine of the ten earn less than the mean, and the top channel earns about 92% of the total.

So a headline such as "the average YouTuber makes $X" usually says little about a typical channel. Ask whether it is a mean or a median, how the channels were chosen, and whether it counts sponsorships. Many creators also earn from sponsorships, memberships and affiliate links, but those depend on deals and audience trust, and no honest formula prices them from view counts.

## Getting paid: the threshold and the wait

Partner Program ad earnings are paid through AdSense. A payment is issued only when your balance reaches the payment threshold, usually $100 or a local-currency equivalent, and otherwise rolls forward. Payments cover the previous month's finalized earnings, so Studio's estimate can shift before the month closes.

Views needed for the first $100 are 100 ÷ RPM × 1,000: 66,667 at a $1.50 RPM, 33,333 at $3 and 16,667 at $6. You also need verified payment and tax details. Depending on your country and any tax treaty, tax can be withheld on revenue from US viewers, so check YouTube's tax pages and, where the amounts justify it, a qualified adviser.

## Can ad revenue replace a job?

Take a $15 hourly wage. The [hourly to salary calculator](/tools/hourly-to-salary-calculator) uses 2,080 hours for a full-time year, so $15 × 2,080 = $31,200 a year, or $2,600 a month. At a $3 RPM, matching that from ads alone takes 31,200 ÷ 3 × 1,000 = 10.4 million views a year, about 866,700 a month. At $6 it is 5.2 million a year.

Early on, the math is harsher. Four videos a month at 10,000 views each is 40,000 views, or $120 at a $3 RPM. If each video takes an assumed 10 hours to make, that is $3 per hour.

## Common mistakes

- **Multiplying views by CPM.** The example above overstated income about 2.6 times.
- **Forecasting from one viral video.** A spike can have an unusual audience mix. Plan from several months of RPM.
- **Mixing up daily and monthly views.** 10,000 views a month is about 330 a day, so entering it as daily overstates income thirtyfold.
- **Trusting third-party estimators.** They cannot see your revenue or geography, so they apply a guessed RPM to public view counts.
- **Assuming every view counts toward eligibility.** Watch hours and Shorts views follow specific rules, so check your Studio progress.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much do small YouTubers make?

Below the Partner Program thresholds, nothing from YouTube ads, though sponsorships or affiliate income are possible. After joining, income is views ÷ 1,000 × RPM, so 10,000 monthly views at a $3 RPM is $30.

### How much do YouTubers make per 1,000 views?

That figure is the RPM. Public roundups commonly quote about $2 to $10 for long-form video, with wide variation by niche and audience country. Your own Studio RPM is the only number that applies to your channel.

### Does YouTube pay per view or per click?

Neither directly. Creators receive a share of net ad revenue from ads that advertisers paid for on their videos. A viewer clicking an ad is not what pays you, and clicking your own ads violates Google's policies.

### How much does a YouTuber with 1 million subscribers make?

Subscribers do not set income, views do. A channel with one million subscribers might get 50,000 views per video or 2 million. As a scale reference, 1,000,000 monthly views at a $3 RPM is $3,000.

### Do YouTubers get paid for Shorts?

Yes, through a different pooled system with its own eligibility route, and per-view revenue is commonly reported as far lower than long-form. Check YouTube's current Shorts monetization policies.
