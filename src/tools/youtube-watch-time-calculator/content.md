## What this YouTube watch time calculator does

This YouTube watch time calculator converts the numbers you already have into the numbers the YouTube Partner Program asks for. Enter your views and average view duration and it returns watch hours. Enter a goal and it returns the views you need. Enter your daily views and it projects the date you will pass each watch-hour requirement. A fourth tab covers the separate Shorts route, which is counted in views rather than hours.

Four thousand hours sounds abstract until you turn it into views. At an average view duration of 3 minutes 36 seconds it is about 66,667 views, and at 8 minutes it is 30,000.

The requirements come from a dated list, checked on September 29, 2026, and the page switches to the announced thresholds on their start date, February 1, 2027. The tool is not affiliated with or endorsed by YouTube or Google, and it never asks for your channel URL or a login.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Pick a tab.** "Hours" turns views into watch hours. "Views needed" turns a goal into a view count and a daily pace. "Timeline" projects the date you reach each requirement. "Shorts" tracks the Shorts views route.
2. **Set your average view duration.** With "From video length", enter the "Video length" (for example 8:00) and the "Average percentage viewed" (for example 45). With "Type it in", enter the duration as Studio shows it, such as 3:36. A plain number is read as minutes, and forms like 4m 30s also work. The two example buttons load an 8-minute and a 20-minute video.
3. **Choose the "Watch-hour requirement".** The list shows the ad-revenue bar in force today, the announced higher bar while it is still ahead, fan-funding early access, or a custom number of hours.
4. **Enter your figure.** Views accepts 25,000, 25000, 25k or 1.2m. On the Views needed tab, set "Finish within (days)". On the Timeline tab, choose "Per day" or "Per month" and add the "Watch hours you have now" and "Monthly growth in views" if you know them.
5. **Read and export.** Results update as you type. "Copy summary" copies plain text, "Download CSV" saves the table, "Load example" restores the starting values and "Clear all" empties the fields.

A field with a problem shows a message under it, and the results wait until it is fixed.

## The formula, with worked examples

Watch time is views multiplied by how long each view lasts. The calculator works in seconds and converts to hours at the end.

```
watch hours = views × average view duration (seconds) ÷ 3,600
views needed = goal hours × 3,600 ÷ average view duration (seconds), rounded up
```

**Example 1: an 8-minute video.** Viewers watch 45% of it on average, so the average view duration is 8:00 × 45% = 3:36, or 216 seconds. Four thousand hours is 14,400,000 seconds. Divide by 216 and you get 66,666.7, which rounds up to 66,667 views.

**Example 2: a 20-minute video.** At 40% viewed the average view duration is 8:00, or 480 seconds. The same goal needs 14,400,000 ÷ 480 = 30,000 views. The second video is 2.5 times as long, yet needs 55% fewer views, because what counts is minutes watched per view, not the length of the file.

**Example 3: the timeline.** A channel getting 500 views a day at 3:36 earns 500 × 216 ÷ 3,600 = 30 watch hours a day. The 4,000-hour bar arrives on day 134, because 133 days gives 3,990 hours and day 134 gives 4,020. The 8,000-hour bar arrives on day 267, and 3,000 hours on day 100.

## How the 365-day window changes the timeline

YouTube looks at the last 365 days, not your lifetime total. Hours you earned a year and a day ago no longer count, so the requirement is a rate you must sustain, not a pile you can collect slowly.

That sets a floor. Four thousand hours spread over 365 days is 10.96 watch hours a day, so a channel earning 10 hours a day tops out at 3,650 and never qualifies. At 3:36 the floor is 183 views a day for 4,000 hours and 366 for 8,000. The Timeline tab shows it as "Minimum steady pace" and warns you when your pace sits below it.

If you enter "Watch hours you have now", the "Drop hours after 365 days" switch decides how they are treated. On, it assumes they were earned evenly over the past year and removes them day by day. Off, it adds new hours to a fixed starting pile, which is optimistic. With 2,000 hours banked and 30 new hours a day, switching it on moves the 4,000-hour date from day 67 to day 82.

## What counts as watch hours, and what does not

**Counts:** public watch time on long-form videos, and on public live streams that stay on the channel as videos, within the last 365 days.

**Does not count:**

- Watch time from Shorts viewed in the Shorts feed, even when the Shorts are public.
- Private and unlisted videos.
- Deleted videos, whose watch time leaves the total.
- Videos watched as ads.
- Traffic YouTube treats as invalid, such as bots or bought views.

Paying for views or watch hours is also against YouTube's rules and puts the channel at risk. For the official figure, open the monetization page in YouTube Studio, which shows the valid public watch hours YouTube uses.

## Ways to raise average view duration

Average view duration moves the views needed sharply. Going from 3:36 to 4:30 cuts the 4,000-hour target from 66,667 views to 53,334.

- **Put the payoff early.** Say what the viewer will get in the first seconds and cut the long preamble.
- **Read the audience retention graph.** In Studio, find where the line drops sharply and tighten that section in the next video.
- **Match the promise.** A title or thumbnail that oversells sends viewers away in the first minute.
- **Judge length in minutes, not percent.** A 20-minute video at 30% viewed gives 6:00. A 10-minute video at 50% gives 5:00. The longer one still delivers more watch time per view.
- **Link videos together.** End screens and playlists move a viewer to another video, and each extra view adds hours. The [words to time converter](/tools/words-to-time) helps you script to a runtime you can hold.

## Who uses this calculator

**New creators planning a schedule.** Suppose each video collects about 1,500 views in its first year at 3:36. Then 66,667 views means about 45 videos. It is hypothetical arithmetic, but it shows whether the plan is weeks or years long.

**Podcasters who post full episodes.** At a 25-minute average view duration, 4,000 hours takes 9,600 views. The same goal at a 4-minute average takes 60,000.

**Channel managers for small businesses.** Use the custom goal to set a quarterly target and the Timeline tab to check whether the current pace can hold it. Once you pass the hours, the [YouTube money calculator](/tools/youtube-money-calculator) estimates what the views might earn.

**Creators weighing Shorts against long-form.** The Shorts tab shows the views per day a 90-day window demands, which is 111,112 a day for 10 million.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Use long-form numbers.** A channel-wide average that includes Shorts is lower than the long-form figure and overstates the views you need.
- **Enter the last 365 days, not all time.** Older views no longer count.
- **Do not confuse percentage viewed with duration.** A 45% average on an 8-minute video is 3:36, not 45 seconds. The "From video length" mode does the multiplication.
- **Expect one spike to expire.** A viral video lifts the total, then leaves the window a year later. Model a steady rate unless you know more.
- **Add a margin.** Reaching the hours is not approval. You also need the subscribers, a clean policy record and an application, and YouTube reviews it. The calculator does not model review time.
- **Treat growth as a guess.** A 5% monthly rise in daily views shortens the timeline, so try 0% first. The [percentage calculator](/tools/percentage-calculator) helps you turn progress into a target.

## Privacy and limitations

Nothing you type leaves this page. What you enter is held in sessionStorage for the current tab, so it survives a reload and is discarded when the tab closes. There is no channel lookup, so every number is one you entered and the tool never sees your real analytics.

It has real limits. It uses a single average view duration for every video, while real channels have videos that perform very differently. The projection is a straight line or a constant monthly growth rate, and it ignores seasonality, algorithm changes and deleted videos. The expiry option assumes your current hours were earned evenly over the past year. It stops at five years, and it cannot see your subscriber count, your policy status or YouTube's review time.

The threshold list is updated by hand and may lag an announcement. It was last checked on September 29, 2026.
