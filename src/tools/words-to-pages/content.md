## What the words to pages calculator does

How many pages is 1000 words? There is no single answer, because a page is not a fixed amount of text. It depends on the typeface, font size, line spacing, margins and paper. In Times New Roman 12 pt with 1 inch margins on US Letter, 1,000 words fill about 1.5 pages single-spaced and about 2.9 pages double-spaced. Change any one setting and the answer moves.

This calculator converts words to pages and pages to words for the format you choose, and shows the same text at single, 1.15, 1.5 and double spacing side by side. A third mode measures pasted text line by line. Nothing is uploaded: the layout is worked out in your browser as you type.

## How to use it

1. **Pick a mode.** "Words to pages" converts a word count, "Pages to words" works backward from a page limit, and "Paste text" measures your actual writing.
2. **Enter a number or paste text.** Counts such as 1000, 1,500 and 80k work, and pages can be decimals such as 2.5.
3. **Set the document format.** Choose the typeface, font size, line spacing, margins and paper (US Letter or A4). Keep "Indented, no gap" for an essay with first-line indents, or pick a block style if paragraphs are separated by a gap.
4. **Read the answer.** The large figure is the result for your format. The tiles show whole pages (rounded up), words per page and lines per page, the row of small pages shows how full the last page is, and the table repeats the answer for all four line spacings.
5. **Use your own figure if you have one.** If an assignment says "assume 300 words per page", type 300 into "Words per page (optional)" and it replaces the layout model.
6. **Use the lookup table** for common lengths at the same format. Copy it or download it as a CSV file.

In Paste text mode every line break starts a paragraph and blank lines are ignored, because the paragraph style already models the gap. If your text came from a PDF or an email that breaks every line, run it through the [remove line breaks tool](/tools/remove-line-breaks) first, or the page count comes out too high.

## How the calculation works

A word processor places text in three steps, and so does this tool.

1. **Text area.** Paper size minus margins. With 1 inch margins, US Letter (8.5 × 11 inches) leaves 6.5 × 9 inches, which is 468 × 648 points (72 points to an inch). A4 is 210 × 297 mm.
2. **Lines per page.** Line height is the typeface's built-in line height times the font size times the spacing factor. The built-in height is about 1.15 times the size for Times New Roman and Arial, 1.22 for Calibri and 1.13 for Courier New, so Times New Roman 12 pt is 13.8 points single and 27.6 double. Lines per page is the text height divided by the line height, rounded down: 648 ÷ 27.6 = 23.5, so 23 lines.
3. **Words per line.** Every character has an advance width in each typeface. The calculator adds up the widths and fills each line until the next word would cross the right margin. In Times New Roman 12 pt a 6.5 inch line holds roughly 14 to 15 words of ordinary prose.

Multiply and you have words per page: about 14.8 words × 23 lines is roughly 340 on a double-spaced page, and 46 single-spaced lines make roughly 680.

**Worked example.** For 1,000 words at the default format, 1,000 ÷ 340 = 2.94 pages double-spaced (three pages needed) and 1,000 ÷ 680 = 1.47 single-spaced (two pages).

When you type a word count, the calculator cannot see your wording, so it lays out a reference passage of English prose (six paragraphs, about five letters per word) over many pages and divides the words by the pages it fills. Paste text mode lays out your own text instead.

| Words | Single-spaced | Double-spaced | Double-spaced, whole pages |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 500 | 0.74 | 1.5 | 2 |
| 1,000 | 1.5 | 2.9 | 3 |
| 2,000 | 2.9 | 5.9 | 6 |
| 5,000 | 7.4 | 14.7 | 15 |

Format: Times New Roman 12 pt, 1 inch margins, US Letter, indented paragraphs.

Reading the table: a 500-word essay fills a page and a half when double-spaced, so plan on two pages. A 1,000-word piece needs three, a 2,000-word article about six, and a 5,000-word report about fifteen. Single-spaced, the same lengths take one page, two, three and eight.

The reference passage has an average word length of about five letters, which is typical of general non-fiction. Dense academic or legal writing, with long words and citations, averages closer to six and fits roughly 10 to 15 percent fewer words on a page. Light, conversational writing fits a few percent more. If your writing is unusually dense, paste it into Paste text mode, or enter your own figure.

## Words per page by typeface

With 1 inch margins on US Letter and indented paragraphs:

| Typeface and size | Single | 1.5 lines | Double |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Times New Roman 12 pt | 680 | 458 | 340 |
| Times New Roman 11 pt | 788 | 525 | 386 |
| Arial 12 pt | 615 | 414 | 307 |
| Arial 11 pt | 723 | 482 | 355 |
| Calibri 12 pt | 624 | 411 | 312 |
| Calibri 11 pt | 741 | 494 | 371 |
| Courier New 12 pt | 467 | 308 | 228 |

Courier New gives every character the same wide slot, so a Courier page holds about a third fewer words than a Times New Roman page at the same size.

Starting from the default (340 words per double-spaced page), here is what one change does:

| Change | Words per double-spaced page |
| --- | --- |
| Font size 11 pt | 386 |
| Font size 13 pt | 286 |
| Margins 0.75 inch | 371 |
| Margins 1.25 inch | 300 |
| A4 paper instead of Letter | 348 |
| Block paragraphs with an 8 pt gap | 325 |
| Block paragraphs with a blank line between | 302 |

Font size and margins are the big levers, which is why adjusting them to reach a page limit is easy to spot and usually breaks the rules.

## Why the 500 and 250 rule of thumb is off

You will often read that a page holds 500 words single-spaced or 250 double-spaced, so 1,000 words is two pages or four. That shortcut comes from typewriter pages, where every character took the same width. The Courier New row matches it: about 230 words on a double-spaced page, in line with the 200 to 250 that manuscript guides tend to quote.

Proportional typefaces fit more letters on a line, so 1,000 words take 2.9 pages in Times New Roman but 4.4 in Courier New. The shortcut is still a safe way to check you have written enough. If your instructor works to it, type 250 into the optional words-per-page field.

## Essays, dissertations and manuscripts

**Essays.** APA Style student papers are double-spaced with 1 inch margins and a half-inch first-line indent, and accept 12 pt Times New Roman, 11 pt Calibri or Arial, and a few other fonts. Those fonts differ by about ten percent in words per page, so a five-page limit means roughly 1,700 to 1,850 words of body text, less if the title page and references count.

**Dissertations and theses.** Universities usually cap these by words and often fix the line spacing (1.5 or double) and a wider binding margin. The calculator uses one margin on all sides, so pick the widest your handbook asks for.

**Manuscripts.** Standard manuscript format is double-spaced 12 pt text with 1 inch margins and a half-inch indent, traditionally in Courier and now often in Times New Roman. Agents and editors judge length by word count. An 80,000-word novel runs to about 235 double-spaced Times New Roman pages, or about 350 in Courier New; published books use smaller pages, so their page counts are not comparable.

## Why page counts differ between Word, Google Docs and print

Two programs rarely agree to the page. Usual causes:

- **A different font.** If a typeface is not installed, the program substitutes one with different widths. Recent Microsoft 365 installs default to Aptos rather than Calibri. Aptos is not modeled here, so pick the closest typeface or enter your own words-per-page figure.
- **In-between spacing.** Templates often start with 1.08 or 1.15 line spacing plus space after each paragraph. Use the 1.15 option and a block paragraph style to approximate them.
- **Headings, tables, images and footnotes** take space without adding many words, so a real document runs longer than plain prose.
- **Layout rules.** Hyphenation moves words between lines, and widow and orphan control can push a stranded line onto the next page. Together they shift a long document by a few percent.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Round up.** A partly filled page is still a page, so plan for the whole-page figure.
- **Count only what is assessed.** Title pages, abstracts and reference lists are often excluded, so paste only the body text.
- **Expect long words to cost space.** Technical writing and citations such as "(Rodriguez et al., 2021)" fit fewer words on a page than plain prose. Paste text mode accounts for this.
- **Prefer word limits to page limits** when you have the choice, because pages depend on the settings of whoever opens the file. The [word counter](/tools/word-counter) gives an exact count, [words to time](/tools/words-to-time) turns it into reading or speaking time, and the [character counter](/tools/character-counter) handles character limits.

## Privacy and limitations

Everything runs in your browser. The text you paste is not uploaded. Your format choices are remembered in this browser, and the current text only until the tab closes.

The result is an estimate. The model uses one typeface and size throughout, does not apply hyphenation or widow and orphan control, and cannot see headings, tables, images or footnotes. Letter widths come from open fonts that are metric-compatible with the four typefaces listed, so lines should break where they do in the originals; other typefaces are not modeled. For languages written without spaces, such as Chinese or Japanese, use a character-based limit.
